PUMP P-101 / TANK LEVEL ANALYSIS — 11 DAYS (Jan 1-11, 2026)
======================================================================

OVERALL HEALTH: GOOD, with ONE significant abnormal event on Jan 5

--- Normal Operation (Days 1-4, 6-11) ---
  Pump cycling pattern: CLEAN and consistent
  Turn-on events per day: ~20-21
  Average run time:    ~30 minutes
  Average rest time:   ~37 minutes
  Level swing per cycle: ~44 (low) -> ~80 (high), delta ~36 units
  Pump ON duty cycle:  ~55%
  No short cycling (< 2 min) on any normal day

--- ABNORMAL EVENT: Day 5 (Jan 5, 2026) ---
  From ~04:17 AM onward, pump entered RAPID CYCLING mode
  174 out of 216 segments (< 2 min each) = 80% short cycling
  Pump on/off every 40-105 seconds
  Level trapped in narrow band: 76-79 (vs normal 44-80)
  Pump ran ~80% of the day during this period
  Short cycling ended sometime after 04:17 and continued
  for the remainder of the day (through end of data)

ROOT CAUSE ASSESSMENT:
  The pump was filling the tank normally at start of day 5.
  Around 04:00, the level was at ~66 when pump kicked in
  (instead of the usual ~44). The pump ran and reached ~79.
  Then it appears the control strategy changed — instead of
  letting the level drop back down to 44 before restarting,
  the pump began rapidly cycling between 76-79 to try to
  maintain a tight level band. This is called short cycling
  or hunting.

POTENTIAL CAUSES:
  1. Control system setpoint/band was changed (operator or
     automated schedule)
  2. Inflow rate increased, so pump could never bring level
     down to 44, and the controller tried trimming around 77
  3. Pump capacity issue (wear, partial blockage)
  4. Level sensor drift or noise in the 76-79 range

RISKS:
  - Short cycling causes excessive motor/starter wear
  - 174 short cycles in one day = high mechanical stress
  - Level never dropped below ~44 during the event, so the
    tank was not in danger of running dry
  - Level did not exceed ~81 during the event, so no overflow

RECOMMENDATIONS:
  1. Check PLC/control panel for any setpoint changes on
     Jan 5 around 04:00 AM
  2. Review if there was a scheduled mode change
  3. Inspect pump for any performance degradation
  4. Verify level transmitter calibration
  5. Check if inflow rates changed (upstream operations)
  6. Restore wide band (44-80) if a narrow band was
     intentionally set — short cycling is bad practice
